The CIE XYZ (1931) system forms the foundation of all [Colourimetry](colourimetry.md). It is designed such that all visible colors can be represented using only positive values, with the Y value representing [Luminance](luminance.md). As a result, the colors corresponding directly to the XYZ primaries are not perceptible. The chromaticity diagram for this system is notably non-linear, meaning that the perceptual difference between two chromaticities is not consistently proportional across the spectrum. In this system, a color is specified by its x and y coordinates, referred to as Yxy. A third coordinate, z, can also be defined, but it is redundant since the sum of x, y, and z equals 1 for all colors.

Additional information: The CIE XYZ system was specifically created to represent color in a way that is device-independent, meaning it provides a standardized color space that can be used universally across different devices and applications. It was derived from experiments with human observers, and it remains one of the most important color spaces in both science and industry. The chromaticity diagram derived from the CIE XYZ system is particularly valuable for understanding color relationships and differences, which is why it is widely used in color science.

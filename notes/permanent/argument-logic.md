An **Argument** in logic is a sequence of statements, called premises, followed by a final statement called the conclusion, which is claimed to follow from the premises.

For example:

- If it's raining, the ground is wet.
- It's raining.
- Therefore, the ground is wet.

An argument is a [Valid Argument](valid-argument.md) if the conclusion must be true whenever all the premises are true. Validity depends only on the form of the argument, not on whether the premises are actually true. See also [Propositional Logic](propositional-logic.md) and [Predicate Logic](predicate-logic.md).

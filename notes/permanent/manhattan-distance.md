**Manhattan Distance** is the distance between two points when you can only travel along axes at right angles. It's the sum of the absolute differences of their coordinates:

$d(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q}) = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} |p_i - q_i|$

It's named after the grid layout of streets in Manhattan, where you can't cut diagonally through the blocks. It's also called taxicab distance or L1 distance.

See [L1 Norm](l1-norm.md). Compare with [Euclidean Distance](euclidean-distance.md).
